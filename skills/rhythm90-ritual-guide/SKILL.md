# Rhythm90 Ritual Guide

Use this skill in an assistant that can read Markdown instructions. It does not require the Rhythm90 website, an API key, a specific model, or another skill. Read [the method reference](references/method.md) for Rhythm90 vocabulary and evidence standards.

## Working approach

1. Identify the user's intended decision or artifact. Read the context they provided before asking questions.
2. Useful context may include: ritual type, team type, top challenges, focus areas. Treat these as prompts for judgment, not a mandatory form. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the answer. A provisional draft is appropriate when its assumptions are labeled.
3. Distinguish observed evidence (with its source and date when supplied), interpretation, and proposed action. Never invent customer research, sources, budgets, targets, owners, or completed work.
4. Apply the method below. Challenge vague claims constructively, explain the improvement, and retain the user's actual constraints.
5. Produce an editable Markdown artifact. Include unresolved questions and the next decision. When asked to save, use a user-selected destination and available tools; otherwise return the artifact in chat. Never claim persistent memory, scheduled work, or an external update without a confirmed tool result.

## Method

Rituals create the temporal structure that keeps strategy alive through the “messy middle.” **Kickoff** commits the plays. **Pulse Checks** (~15 min, slide-free) ask what’s live/stuck and what surprised us — never a status tour. **R&R** synthesizes learning and replaces QBR theater.

Rhythm90 Lead mantra: *Create rhythm, not theater.* Start Pulse Checks with “Any surprises this week?”

- Keep shared time conversational and decision-oriented. Use a short evidence pre-read and the team’s existing formats when they help the decision.
- Kickoff: force hypothesis clarity, owners, signal plan — not brainstorm theater.
- Pulse Check: live / stuck / early signals / unblock — cut defensive status reports.
- R&R: what ran, what we learned, what to retire/pause, what carries forward — not vanity win decks.
- Keep the agenda within the available time. Send detailed troubleshooting to a focused follow-up with an owner.
- Distinguish immediate operational alerts from outcomes that need their agreed observation window.
- After a session, separate accepted commitments from suggestions. Preserve unresolved disagreement and the next evidence check; never invent agreement or an owner’s acceptance.

## Useful output

Use the relevant fields below. Omit irrelevant sections; mark missing evidence explicitly:

- **Ritual Type**
- **Main Goal**
- **Checklist** — ordered list / timed agenda
- **Success Tips**
- **Assumption Check**
- **Quarterly Link**
- **Facilitation Questions** — sharp prompts for the lead

## Evidence and review

Trace conclusions to the provided inputs. A plausible explanation is still a hypothesis. Include counter-evidence or alternative explanations when they could change the decision. Do not convert AI-simulated audiences into actual customer evidence. Decisions to fund, scale, or retire work remain recommendations until the practitioner accepts them.

Keep output proportional to the task. A short field note or one-page canvas is usually better than a sprawling report. Avoid jargon and fabricated certainty. If the user is studying, explain the reasoning and offer one exercise rather than doing all the thinking for them.

## Worked behavior check

Request: We have 15 minutes and two stuck Plays. Help run a Pulse Check.

Expected approach: Prepare a timed agenda that identifies blockers, assigns owners, captures new evidence, and defers detailed troubleshooting. Do not generate a full quarterly planning workshop.

Before returning the artifact, check: Is the evidence real and traceable? Are assumptions visible? Is there a clear next decision? Does this help learning rather than simply add output?
